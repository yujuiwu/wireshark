What the graph answers

The WLAN Block Ack Graph turns BA responses, reverse-direction BARs, and matching QoS Data frames into a capture-relative time versus sequence-number view. It helps you see how the receive window moved, which bitmap positions were set, whether zero positions persisted, and what evidence lies inside the current viewport.

Interpretation boundary: a bitmap zero or an SSN-advance gap does not prove an MPDU was transmitted or lost. A captured MPDU does not prove reception. Treat every overlay as capture evidence, not a standalone loss verdict.

Read each feature

BA pair and TID selectors

The BA pair (TA → RA) list is sorted by the total received BA count summed across every TID, highest first; each item shows that total and its number of TIDs. Within a pair, the TID list is also sorted by received BA count, highest first, and shows its BA and BAR counts. Address order and numeric TID order break ties. A session matching the selected packet remains selected; otherwise the busiest pair and TID are the defaults.

Core BA, window, and bitmap-set traces

The blue step trace is the BA SSN. The orange trace is the exclusive receive-window upper bound: unwrapped SSN plus the number of bitmap sequence positions. Green dots are set positions. Clear Show bitmap set when dense green evidence hides the core traces; the analysis and summary counts remain unchanged. See the normal and holes screenshots.

Explicit FCS errors

When Radiotap explicitly flags a bad FCS or Wireshark calculates a bad WLAN FCS, the affected BA marker, window marker, bitmap dots or crosses, and SSN label are gray. Blue and orange step lines retain their session colors because each segment spans more than one sample. Missing, unchecked, or unavailable FCS status is not treated as an error. The BA remains in the analysis, and its detail text and the viewport summary report the condition. Select Bad-FCS BA bitmap in the interactive graph to see two real bad-FCS BAs.

SSN and BA timing labels

Show SSN labels adds wrapped 12-bit values below the BA points. Show BA time deltas adds elapsed time from the previous BA above each blue segment. Labels annotate the graph only. The normal screenshot shows both.

BARs and captured MPDUs

Purple, white-filled triangles identify reverse-direction BAR starting sequences. Optional QoS Data observations use gold diamonds with dark outlines, making them distinct by color and shape. A-MPDU members are plotted separately. They do not change BA analysis. See the normal screenshot.

Bitmap holes and persistent aging

Crosses are zero positions before the highest set position in one BA; trailing zeros are not plotted. A green cross means an earlier BA explicitly set that sequence in the same agreement. A red cross means the graph has no earlier observed set-bit evidence for it. Successful ADDBA responses, DELBA frames, and inferred analysis-epoch resets clear that history. Neither color proves transmission or loss. An explicit bad-FCS overlay takes precedence and turns either kind of cross gray, while the summary retains the underlying classification. A dark-red span means a red position remained zero across at least two consecutive covering BAs. Its square endpoint reports age and outcome. The holes screenshot shows the red and persistent layers.

No BA ACK before SSN advance

Gray dots appear when a later BA SSN passes sequence numbers for which no BA acknowledgment was observed. This is missing BA evidence—not proof of transmission or loss. Turn the layer on in the normal scenario to inspect 35 real gap positions.

Rectangle zoom and live summary

Select Zoom and drag a rectangle over the plot, use the wheel, or use the X/Y controls. The summary recomputes BA, BAR, MPDU, set, hole, persistent-span, and gap counts for the X/Y viewport. X-axis duration is the displayed axis span, including margins—not simply the first-to-last event time.

12-bit sequence wrapping

The graph unwraps progress internally, so 4095→0 continues upward. Tick and SSN labels remain modulo 4096. In the wrap screenshot, SSN 0 is plotted internally at 4096. Old backward samples that cannot extend the acknowledgment frontier begin a new analysis epoch.

Selection, reset, and export

Click a marker for its type, normalized time, wrapped and unwrapped sequence, and relevant bitmap or lifetime facts. In Wireshark, selection also moves the packet list to the source frame. Reset Graph fits the active session; Save As… exports the plot as PDF, PNG, BMP, or JPEG.
